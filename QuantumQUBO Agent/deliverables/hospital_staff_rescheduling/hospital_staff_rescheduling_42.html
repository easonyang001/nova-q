Hospital Staff Rescheduling Problem

Problem Statement

Let $\mathcal{S}$ denote the set of available staff members and $\mathcal{J}$ denote the set of uncovered shifts. Let $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{J}$ be the set of qualified staff-shift pairs, where $(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}$ indicates that staff member $s$ is qualified to cover shift $j$. For each qualified pair $(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}$, let $c_{s,j} \ge 0$ represent the cost of assigning staff $s$ to shift $j$, which may encode fatigue, travel distance, overtime, or preference penalties. The objective is to select a set of assignments such that every shift in $\mathcal{J}$ is covered by exactly one staff member, each staff member covers at most one shift, and the total assignment cost is minimized.

Decision Variables

For each qualified pair $(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}$, introduce a binary decision variable $x_{s,j} \in \{0,1\}$. The variable $x_{s,j}$ takes the value $1$ if staff member $s$ is assigned to shift $j$, and $0$ otherwise. The qualification constraint is enforced by restricting the variable domain to $\mathcal{A}$; variables corresponding to unqualified pairs are excluded from the formulation.

QUBO Derivation

We construct a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) formulation by converting the constrained minimization problem into an unconstrained energy minimization problem $H(\mathbf{x})$. Step 1: Objective Function. The original objective is to minimize the total assignment cost: \begin{align} \min \sum_{(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}} c_{s,j} x_{s,j}. \end{align} This term is retained directly in the QUBO Hamiltonian. Step 2: Constraints. The problem imposes two types of constraints:
  1. Shift Coverage: Each shift $j \in \mathcal{J}$ must be assigned exactly one staff member. Let $\mathcal{S}_j = \{s \in \mathcal{S} : (s,j) \in \mathcal{A}\}$ be the set of qualified staff for shift $j$. The constraint is: \begin{align} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_j} x_{s,j} = 1 \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{J}. \end{align}
  2. Staff Limit: Each staff member $s \in \mathcal{S}$ can cover at most one shift. Let $\mathcal{J}_s = \{j \in \mathcal{J} : (s,j) \in \mathcal{A}\}$ be the set of shifts qualified for staff $s$. The constraint is: \begin{align} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j} \le 1 \quad \forall s \in \mathcal{S}. \end{align}
Step 3: Penalty Terms. We convert constraints to quadratic penalty terms using a penalty weight $\lambda > 0$. Shift Coverage Penalty: For each shift $j$, we enforce equality using the squared penalty: \begin{align} P_{\text{shift},j} = \lambda \left( \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_j} x_{s,j} - 1 \right)^2. \end{align} Expanding using the idempotent property $x_{s,j}^2 = x_{s,j}$ for binary variables: \begin{align} P_{\text{shift},j} &= \lambda \left( \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_j} x_{s,j}^2 - 2 \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_j} x_{s,j} + 1 \right) \\ &= \lambda \left( -\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_j} x_{s,j} + 2 \sum_{\substack{s,s' \in \mathcal{S}_j \\ s < s'}} x_{s,j} x_{s',j} + 1 \right). \end{align} Summing over all shifts gives the total shift penalty $H_{\text{shift}} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}} P_{\text{shift},j}$. Staff Limit Penalty: For each staff member $s$, we enforce the inequality $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j} \le 1$ using the squared penalty $\lambda \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j} \right)^2$, which is zero for sums $0$ or $1$ and positive for sums greater than $1$: \begin{align} P_{\text{staff},s} = \lambda \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j} \right)^2. \end{align} Expanding: \begin{align} P_{\text{staff},s} &= \lambda \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j}^2 + 2 \sum_{\substack{j,j' \in \mathcal{J}_s \\ j < j'}} x_{s,j} x_{s,j'} \right) \\ &= \lambda \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_s} x_{s,j} + 2 \sum_{\substack{j,j' \in \mathcal{J}_s \\ j < j'}} x_{s,j} x_{s,j'} \right). \end{align} Summing over all staff gives the total staff penalty $H_{\text{staff}} = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} P_{\text{staff},s}$. Collecting coefficients for linear and quadratic terms yields the QUBO parameters. The Hamiltonian can be written as $H(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}} Q_{(s,j),(s,j)} x_{s,j} + \sum_{(s,j) \neq (s',j') \in \mathcal{A}^2} Q_{(s,j),(s',j')} x_{s,j} x_{s',j'} + c$. The entries are: \begin{align} Q_{(s,j),(s,j)} &= c_{s,j} - \lambda + \lambda = c_{s,j}, \\ Q_{(s,j),(s',j)} &= 2\lambda \quad \text{for } s \neq s', \\ Q_{(s,j),(s,j')} &= 2\lambda \quad \text{for } j \neq j', \\ Q_{(s,j),(s',j')} &= 0 \quad \text{otherwise}, \\ c &= 0. \end{align} Here, $Q_{(s,j),(s',j)}$ corresponds to pairs sharing the same shift, and $Q_{(s,j),(s,j')}$ corresponds to pairs sharing the same staff member.

Penalty Weight Justification

The penalty weight $\lambda$ must be chosen sufficiently large to ensure that any violation of the constraints results in an energy increase that outweighs any potential reduction in the objective cost. Specifically, let $C_{\max} = \max_{(s,j) \in \mathcal{A}} c_{s,j}$. Choosing $\lambda > C_{\max}$ guarantees that the penalty for violating a constraint exceeds the maximum possible gain from the objective function. For the shift coverage constraint, a violation allows at most saving $C_{\max}$ in cost (by omitting an assignment), while incurring a penalty of at least $\lambda$. For the staff limit constraint, a violation incurs a penalty of at least $\lambda$ with no cost reduction. Thus, $\lambda > C_{\max}$ ensures that infeasible solutions have strictly higher energy than the best feasible solution.

Correctness Argument

For any feasible solution, the penalty terms vanish (after offset subtraction), so $H(\mathbf{x})$ equals the total assignment cost. For any infeasible solution, at least one penalty term is positive, contributing at least $\lambda$ to the energy. Since $\lambda > C_{\max}$, the energy of any infeasible solution exceeds the cost of any feasible solution (assuming costs are non-negative and at least one feasible assignment exists). Therefore, the global minimum of $H(\mathbf{x})$ must be a feasible solution with minimum total cost, corresponding to the optimal staff rescheduling.
