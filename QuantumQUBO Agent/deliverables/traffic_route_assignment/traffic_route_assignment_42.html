Traffic Route Assignment Problem

Problem Statement

Given a set of $N$ cars and a set of candidate routes for each car, assign exactly one route to each car so as to minimize the total congestion cost. The congestion cost is defined as the total number of pairs of selected routes that share at least one common road segment.

Decision Variables

Let $x_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$ denote the binary decision variable for car $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ and candidate route $j \in \{0, \dots, M_i-1\}$, where $x_{ij} = 1$ if car $i$ is assigned route $j$, and $x_{ij} = 0$ otherwise. Let $y_{js} \in \{0,1\}$ be a known parameter indicating whether route $j$ traverses road segment $s \in \mathcal{S}$, where $\mathcal{S}$ is the set of all road segments.

QUBO Derivation

Step 1 --- Objective formulation. Let $C_s$ be the number of selected routes that use segment $s$. This is given by $C_s = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} y_{js} x_{ij}$. The total overlap cost counts each pair of selected routes sharing segment $s$ exactly once, which is $\frac{1}{2} C_s (C_s - 1)$. Summing over all segments, the objective to minimize is \begin{align} H_{\text{obj}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \frac{1}{2} C_s (C_s - 1) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \left( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} \sum_{i'=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j'=0}^{M_i-1} y_{js} y_{j's} x_{ij} x_{i'j'} - \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} y_{js} x_{ij} \right). \end{align} Step 2 --- Constraint formulation. Each car must be assigned exactly one route. For every car $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$, this is expressed as \begin{align} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} = 1. \end{align} Step 3 --- Penalty term conversion. We enforce the constraints by adding a quadratic penalty for each car. Let $\lambda > 0$ be the penalty weight. The penalty for car $i$ is \begin{align} P_i(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda \left( \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} - 1 \right)^2. \end{align} Expanding the square and applying the idempotent property $x_{ij}^2 = x_{ij}$ for binary variables yields \begin{align} P_i(\mathbf{x}) &= \lambda \left( \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij}^2 - 2 \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} + 1 + \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} \sum_{\substack{j'=0 \\ j' \neq j}}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} x_{ij'} \right) \\ &= \lambda \left( 1 - \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} + \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} \sum_{\substack{j'=0 \\ j' \neq j}}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} x_{ij'} \right). \end{align} Summing over all cars gives the total penalty contribution to the Hamiltonian: \begin{align} H_{\text{pen}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} P_i(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda N - \lambda \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} + \lambda \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j=0}^{M_i-1} \sum_{\substack{j'=0 \\ j' \neq j}}^{M_i-1} x_{ij} x_{ij'}. \end{align} Step 4 --- Combined QUBO Hamiltonian. Adding the objective and penalty terms yields the full QUBO energy function $H(\mathbf{x}) = H_{\text{obj}}(\mathbf{x}) + H_{\text{pen}}(\mathbf{x})$: \begin{align} H(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \sum_{i,j} \sum_{i',j'} y_{js} y_{j's} x_{ij} x_{i'j'} - \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \sum_{i,j} y_{js} x_{ij} + \lambda \sum_{i} \sum_{j \neq j'} x_{ij} x_{ij'} - \lambda \sum_{i,j} x_{ij} + \lambda N. \end{align} Step 5 --- Q matrix entries and offset. Grouping quadratic terms $x_{ij} x_{i'j'}$ and linear terms $x_{ij}$, and noting that $y_{js} \in \{0,1\}$ implies $y_{js}^2 = y_{js}$, we read off the QUBO coefficients. The diagonal entries receive contributions from the objective's quadratic self-interaction and the penalty's linear term. The off-diagonal entries receive contributions from the objective's cross-interaction and the penalty's same-car interaction. The resulting closed-form expressions are: \begin{align} Q_{(ij),(i'j')} &= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} y_{js} y_{j's} + \lambda \, \delta_{ii'} (1 - \delta_{jj'}), \\ Q_{(ij),(ij)} &= -\lambda, \\ c &= \lambda N, \end{align} where $\delta_{ab}$ is the Kronecker delta, and indices $(ij)$ and $(i'j')$ range over all valid car-route pairs.

Penalty Weight Justification

The penalty weight $\lambda$ must be chosen sufficiently large to ensure that any infeasible assignment (violating the exactly-one-route constraint) has a strictly higher energy than any feasible assignment. The maximum possible reduction in the objective cost achievable by violating a single constraint is bounded by the maximum possible overlap cost, which is at most the total number of route pairs times the maximum number of shared segments between any two routes. Let $W_{\max}$ denote this upper bound on the objective function over the entire hypercube $\{0,1\}^n$. Choosing $\lambda > W_{\max}$ guarantees that the energy penalty for violating any constraint dominates any potential decrease in the objective function. Consequently, the global minimum of $H(\mathbf{x})$ is guaranteed to lie within the feasible subspace.

Correctness Argument

Minimizing $H(\mathbf{x})$ over $\{0,1\}^n$ recovers the optimal route assignment because $H(\mathbf{x})$ is constructed as the sum of the original objective and non-negative penalty terms. For any feasible $\mathbf{x}$, the constraint terms vanish, so $H(\mathbf{x}) = H_{\text{obj}}(\mathbf{x})$. For any infeasible $\mathbf{x}$, at least one penalty term is strictly positive. By choosing $\lambda$ strictly greater than the maximum possible objective value, the energy increase from constraint violation strictly exceeds any possible decrease in the objective function. Consequently, the global minimum of $H(\mathbf{x})$ must occur at a feasible point that minimizes $H_{\text{obj}}$, which corresponds exactly to the optimal solution of the original combinatorial problem.
