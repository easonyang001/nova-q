VLSI Bisection Placement Problem

Problem Statement

Given a set of $N$ circuit modules and a set of weighted net connections $E$ between them, the VLSI Bisection Placement Problem requires partitioning the modules into two placement regions, $A$ and $B$, such that each region contains exactly $N/2$ modules. The objective is to minimize the total weight of nets that connect a module in region $A$ to a module in region $B$.

Decision Variables

For each module $i \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$, we introduce a binary variable $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$. The value $x_i = 0$ indicates that module $i$ is assigned to region $A$, while $x_i = 1$ indicates assignment to region $B$.

QUBO Derivation

Step 1 --- Write the original objective in general notation. A net $(i,j) \in E$ with weight $w_{ij}$ crosses the bisection if and only if $x_i \neq x_j$. This condition is captured by the algebraic expression $x_i + x_j - 2x_i x_j$, which evaluates to $1$ when $x_i \neq x_j$ and $0$ otherwise. The minimization objective is therefore: \begin{align} \min_{x \in \{0,1\}^N} \sum_{(i,j) \in E} w_{ij} (x_i + x_j - 2x_i x_j). \end{align} Step 2 --- Write each constraint in its original algebraic form. The requirement that both regions contain exactly half of the modules is expressed as: \begin{align} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i = \frac{N}{2}. \end{align} Step 3 --- Convert the constraint to a penalty term. We enforce the constraint by adding a quadratic penalty term with weight $\lambda > 0$: \begin{align} P(x) = \lambda \left( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i - \frac{N}{2} \right)^2. \end{align} Expanding the square and applying the idempotent property $x_i^2 = x_i$ for binary variables yields: \begin{align} P(x) &= \lambda \left( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i^2 - N \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i + \frac{N^2}{4} + \sum_{i \neq j} x_i x_j \right) \\ &= \lambda \left( (1 - N) \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i + \sum_{i \neq j} x_i x_j + \frac{N^2}{4} \right). \end{align} Step 4 --- Combine objective and penalty terms into the single QUBO Hamiltonian $H(x)$: \begin{align} H(x) = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} w_{ij} (x_i + x_j - 2x_i x_j) + \lambda \left( (1 - N) \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} x_i + \sum_{i \neq j} x_i x_j + \frac{N^2}{4} \right). \end{align} Step 5 --- Read off the Q matrix entries and offset $c$ from $H$ in general closed form. Collecting coefficients for each monomial $x_i x_j$ (with $i \leq j$) gives: \begin{align} Q_{i,i} &= \sum_{j : (i,j) \in E} w_{ij} + \lambda(1 - N), \quad \forall i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}, \\ Q_{i,j} &= -2w_{ij} + 2\lambda, \quad \forall i < j \text{ such that } (i,j) \in E, \\ Q_{i,j} &= 2\lambda, \quad \forall i < j \text{ such that } (i,j) \notin E, \\ c &= \lambda \frac{N^2}{4}. \end{align}

Penalty Weight Justification

The penalty coefficient $\lambda$ must be chosen sufficiently large to ensure that any violation of the balance constraint incurs a higher energy cost than the maximum possible reduction in the cut weight. Specifically, let $W_{\max} = \max_{(i,j) \in E} w_{ij}$ denote the maximum net weight. Choosing $\lambda > W_{\max}$ guarantees that the penalty for deviating from the required partition size strictly dominates any objective improvement achievable by an unbalanced assignment.

Minimizing $H(x)$ over $\{0,1\}^N$ recovers the optimal bisection because feasible solutions (those satisfying $\sum_i x_i = N/2$) incur zero penalty and their energy equals the cut weight. Infeasible solutions incur a penalty of at least $\lambda$. Since $\lambda > W_{\max}$, the energy of any infeasible solution exceeds the energy of the best feasible solution by a margin that cannot be offset by reducing the cut weight. Consequently, the global minimum of $H(x)$ must correspond to a feasible assignment that minimizes the total cut weight.
