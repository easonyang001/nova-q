Mars Satellite Observation Scheduling Problem

Problem Statement

Given a set of $N$ candidate observations for a Mars-orbiting instrument, each observation $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ is associated with a science priority score $p_i \ge 0$, a resource usage vector $\mathbf{u}_i \in \mathbb{R}^K_{\ge 0}$ across $K$ distinct resource types, and a set of temporal or instrumental conflicts $C \subseteq \{(i,j) : i \neq j\}$. Each resource type $k \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$ has a total available capacity $R_k > 0$. The goal is to select a subset of observations that maximizes the total science priority score while ensuring that no two selected observations conflict and that the total resource consumption for each type $k$ does not exceed $R_k$.

Decision Variables

For each candidate observation $i \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$, we introduce a binary decision variable $x_i \in \{0,1\}$. The variable $x_i = 1$ indicates that observation $i$ is selected for execution, and $x_i = 0$ indicates it is not.

QUBO Derivation

Step 1 --- The original optimization problem is a maximization. To cast it as a minimization problem suitable for quantum annealing or QUBO solvers, we negate the objective function. The unconstrained minimization objective is: \begin{align} \min_{\mathbf{x} \in \{0,1\}^N} \left( -\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} p_i x_i \right). \end{align} Step 2 --- The problem constraints are expressed in general algebraic form. Conflict avoidance requires that for every conflicting pair $(i,j) \in C$, at most one observation is selected: \begin{align} x_i + x_j \le 1, \quad \forall (i,j) \in C. \end{align} Since $x_i, x_j \in \{0,1\}$, this is equivalent to the quadratic equality constraint $x_i x_j = 0$. Resource capacity limits require that for each resource type $k \in \{0, \dots, K-1\}$: \begin{align} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} u_{i,k} x_i \le R_k. \end{align} Step 3 --- We convert each constraint into a quadratic penalty term. For conflicts, we add a penalty $A > 0$ for every violated pair: \begin{align} H_{\text{conflict}}(\mathbf{x}) = A \sum_{(i,j) \in C} x_i x_j. \end{align} For resource constraints, we use a squared penalty $B > 0$. Penalizing the squared deviation from the limit enforces the constraint when $B$ is sufficiently large: \begin{align} H_{\text{resource}}(\mathbf{x}) = B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \left( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} u_{i,k} x_i - R_k \right)^2. \end{align} Expanding the squared term using the idempotent property $x_i^2 = x_i$ for binary variables: \begin{align} \left( \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} u_{i,k} x_i - R_k \right)^2 = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} u_{i,k}^2 x_i - 2 R_k \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} u_{i,k} x_i + \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \sum_{j \neq i} u_{i,k} u_{j,k} x_i x_j + R_k^2. \end{align} Multiplying by $B$ and summing over $k$, the resource penalty contribution becomes: \begin{align} H_{\text{resource}}(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \left[ B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} (u_{i,k}^2 - 2 R_k u_{i,k}) \right] x_i + \sum_{0 \le i < j \le N-1} \left[ 2 B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} u_{i,k} u_{j,k} \right] x_i x_j + B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} R_k^2. \end{align} Step 4 --- Combining the negated objective, conflict penalty, and resource penalty yields the total QUBO Hamiltonian: \begin{align} H(\mathbf{x}) = -\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} p_i x_i + A \sum_{(i,j) \in C} x_i x_j + H_{\text{resource}}(\mathbf{x}). \end{align} Substituting the expanded resource penalty and grouping terms by monomial: \begin{align} H(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \left[ -p_i + B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} (u_{i,k}^2 - 2 R_k u_{i,k}) \right] x_i + \sum_{0 \le i < j \le N-1} \left[ A \cdot \mathbb{I}((i,j) \in C) + 2 B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} u_{i,k} u_{j,k} \right] x_i x_j + B \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} R_k^2, \end{align} where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function. Penalty Weight Justification

The penalty weights $A$ and $B$ must be chosen sufficiently large to ensure that any violation of the constraints increases the energy more than the maximum possible gain in the objective function. Specifically, let $P_{\max} = \max_i p_i$ and $P_{\text{total}} = \sum_i p_i$. To guarantee that conflict avoidance is strictly enforced, we require $A > P_{\max}$. This ensures that the energy cost of selecting two conflicting observations exceeds the priority score of any single observation. For resource constraints, let $\Delta_{\min}$ be the minimum positive violation amount in resource units. We require $B > P_{\text{total}} / \Delta_{\min}^2$. This guarantees that the quadratic penalty for any resource violation strictly dominates the total possible science priority gain, thereby forcing the global minimum to satisfy all constraints.

Correctness Argument

Minimizing $H(\mathbf{x})$ over $\{0,1\}^N$ recovers the optimal observation schedule because feasible solutions incur zero penalty, reducing $H(\mathbf{x})$ to $-\sum_i p_i x_i + c$. Thus, among feasible solutions, minimizing $H$ is equivalent to maximizing the total priority score. Infeasible solutions incur strictly positive penalty terms. Given the lower bounds on $A$ and $B$, the energy of any infeasible solution exceeds the energy of the best feasible solution. Consequently, the global minimum of $H(\mathbf{x})$ must correspond to a feasible assignment that maximizes the original objective, guaranteeing correctness.
